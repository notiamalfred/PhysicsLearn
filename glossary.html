    Glossary

    Key definitions from the notes in one place.

    Scalar

    A quantity with magnitude only, e.g. distance, speed, mass, density.

    Vector

    A quantity with both magnitude and direction, e.g. displacement, velocity, acceleration, force.

    Resultant

    The single vector produced when two or more vectors are added.

    Distance

    The length of the path taken by an object. It is a scalar.

    Displacement

    The shortest distance from the starting position to the final position. It is a vector and is independent of the path.

    Speed

    Distance travelled per unit time.

    Velocity

    Displacement per unit time; it tells how fast and in which direction an object moves.

    Uniform motion

    Motion with constant velocity: speed and direction both stay unchanged.

    Acceleration

    The rate of change of velocity, a = (v − u) ÷ t. Unit: m s⁻².

    Acceleration due to gravity (g)

    The constant acceleration of an object under gravity only, about 9.81 m s⁻² towards the Earth.

    Air resistance

    The force exerted by the air that opposes the motion of a moving object. It increases with speed.

    Terminal velocity

    The constant velocity reached when the weight of a falling object is balanced by air resistance.

    Data-logging system

    A system of sensors, an interface and computer software that records time-varying data and shows it as graphs and tables.

    Stroboscopy

    A method of capturing images of fast-moving objects using intense flashes at an adjustable frequency.

    Force

    A vector measured in newtons (N). An unbalanced force changes an object's speed and/or direction.

    Net force

    The resultant of all forces acting on an object; also called the unbalanced force.

    Inertia

    The tendency of an object to resist changes in its state of motion. It is measured by mass.

    Friction

    A force opposite to the direction of motion, independent of speed and of the area in contact.

    Weight

    The force exerted on an object by the Earth, always directed vertically downward.

    Normal reaction

    The force perpendicular to the contact surface when two objects press against each other.

    Free-body diagram

    A diagram showing all the forces acting on one object, drawn as arrows.

    Moment of a force

    The turning effect of a force about a point: perpendicular component of the force × distance from the point (N m).

    Couple

    Two equal forces acting in opposite directions. The resultant force is zero but there is a turning effect.

    Torque

    The net moment of force produced by a couple.

    Equilibrium

    A state with no translational or rotational acceleration: zero net force and balanced clockwise and anticlockwise moments.

    Centre of gravity

    The point where all the weight of an object can be considered to be concentrated.
